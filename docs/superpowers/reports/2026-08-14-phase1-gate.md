# Phase 1 gate decision

**Date:** 2026-08-14
**Verdict:** PASS. Proceed to the browser extension.

## Result

Balanced accuracy on generators the training never saw, measured at the 0.65
decision threshold, with the threshold fitted on a disjoint half of the
held-out set:

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| **Balanced accuracy (held-out generators)** | **0.8362** |
| Required to pass | 0.7500 |
| Balanced accuracy (seen generators) | 0.9294 |
| True positive rate (synthetic detected) | 0.8023 |
| True negative rate (real kept) | 0.8700 |
| Low-confidence dead zone | 0.2954 |

Per generator:

| Generator | Accuracy | Note |
|---|---|---|
| nano-banana | 0.9825 | Gemini image model, 2025, never seen in training |
| real | 0.8700 | |
| flux | 0.8533 | rectified-flow transformer, never seen in training |
| biggan | 0.4600 | GAN, never seen in training; the weak point |

Configuration: CLIP ViT-B/32 frozen backbone, one hidden layer of 256 units,
19,400 training images spanning 17 synthetic generators and 8 real sources.

Per the decision rule in the design spec, 0.75 to 0.85 means viable but
requiring the full augmentation program and more data before submission.

## What the measurements actually showed

**The head needed more capacity than assumed.** The spec argued for a linear
head on the grounds that capacity overfits the generators present in
training. That was wrong here. A linear head could not represent GAN
detection and diffusion detection at the same time in CLIP feature space:
adding GAN training data cost seven points on held-out FLUX and bought only
four points on held-out BigGAN, a net loss. One hidden layer holds both.

| Head | GAN data in training | BA | BigGAN | FLUX |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| linear | no | 0.8233 | 0.335 | 0.875 |
| linear | yes | 0.7931 | 0.318 | 0.790 |
| MLP 512 | no | 0.8463 | 0.438 | 0.884 |
| MLP 512 | yes | 0.8446 | 0.532 | 0.869 |

(These figures come from the exploratory sweep, before the calibration split
was introduced, so they run about a point high. The comparison between rows
is what matters.)

GAN data was kept even though it costs a fraction of a point in aggregate,
because it roughly doubles accuracy on the worst generator. Against an unknown
private benchmark, worst-case robustness is worth more than a rounding
difference in the average.

**The threshold was being fitted on the reported images.** The first
implementation fitted the decision threshold on the held-out set and then
reported accuracy on that same set, which chooses the threshold knowing the
answers. The held-out set is now halved per generator: threshold from one
half, number from the other. This cost about one point of reported accuracy
and made the remainder trustworthy.

**Calibrating onto 0.65 is worth roughly two points, not more.** The balanced
accuracy curve peaks at 0.62 to 0.66 and is flat across the top. A
conventionally calibrated model thresholded at 0.65 would land near 0.80
instead of 0.82. Real, but smaller than the design document predicted.

## Known weakness

BigGAN sits at 0.46 while both modern held-out generators clear 0.85. GAN
artifacts do not transfer from the GAN families in training, which are all
face generators (StyleGAN3, StarGAN), to BigGAN's ImageNet-style objects. The
object-GAN datasets that would close this gap were unusable: `sywang/CNNDetection`
has an unparseable split layout and `gojay/StyleGAN2-Face` stores image paths
rather than bytes.

This matters less than the aggregate suggests, because BigGAN is 23% of the
held-out synthetic images here and a far smaller share of AI images actually
encountered on the web, which are overwhelmingly diffusion and transformer
models. It is recorded as a real gap rather than adjusted away.

## Next

Plan B: the Chrome extension and the browser-side evaluation harness. Before
submission, per the decision rule: scale the dataset and confirm the
augmentation program end to end.

Backbone comparison against SigLIP is still open. Its extraction was stopped
after measuring roughly three times the per-image cost of CLIP ViT-B/32,
which is a poor trade in a browser latency budget. It should be revisited on a
small subset rather than the full set.
